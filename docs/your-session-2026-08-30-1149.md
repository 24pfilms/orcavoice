**Yes — three things in OpenWhispr are genuinely worth taking, and it's not the feature list.** It's MIT-licensed, but it's Electron; the valuable parts are two Win32 C files and one algorithm, all of which port to your Tauri shell.

**Take these (high value, Windows-first, transfer cleanly):**

1. **`resources/windows-text-monitor.c`** — real **UI Automation** (`IUIAutomationTextPattern`, `GetSelection`, `focused_has_selection`). This reads the selected text in *any* focused app without touching the clipboard. This is the single best asset in the repo, and it's ~50 lines of `windows-rs` in your Rust shell.
2. **`resources/windows-fast-paste.c`** — **SendInput** Ctrl+V injection, plus their **verified clipboard restore** (write → paste → re-read → only restore if it still matches what you wrote). That guard is pure logic and prevents clobbering a user's copy mid-paste.
3. **`resources/windows-key-listener.c`** — **WH_KEYBOARD_LL** hook for true push-to-talk. You need this because *no* global-shortcut plugin sees key-**up** or distinguishes left/right modifiers. They bypass Electron's own API for exactly this reason.

**Skip these:** their MCP server isn't in the repo (hosted only), their "VAD" is just an RMS level meter, their whisper.cpp/Parakeet builds are **CPU-only on Windows** (no CUDA/Vulkan), and their Linux stereo workaround is PipeWire-specific.

**The OrcaVoice extension I'd actually build**, given you already have `transcribeVoice` (Whisper via transformers.js) in `gg-core`: a **selection-aware voice command** — hold hotkey, UIA reads what's highlighted in any app, your existing agent edits it, SendInput pastes it back in place. That turns OrcaVoice from dictation into *voice-driven editing across every window*, which pairs directly with your multi-window agent model.

One caveat worth knowing before you commit: **SendInput silently fails against elevated/admin windows** (UIPI blocks it) — undocumented in their repo, and all three of their fallbacks hit the same wall.
