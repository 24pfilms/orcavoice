# Voice Commands

Voice commands let you open apps by speaking instead of typing.

## How to use them

1. Hold your hotkey to start recording.
2. Say **"open"** followed by the app name — for example, "open chrome".
3. Release the hotkey.

OrcaVoice launches the app instead of pasting text.

You can also say **"launch"** or **"start"** instead of "open." All three words work the same way.

## What app names work

Say the name your computer uses for the app. On Windows, that means the name you see when you search the Start menu. On Mac, it is the name under the app icon in Applications or Launchpad.

| What you say | What happens |
|---|---|
| open chrome | Opens Google Chrome |
| launch notepad | Opens Notepad (Windows) |
| start terminal | Opens Terminal (Mac) |
| open spotify | Opens Spotify |
| open code | Opens Visual Studio Code |

If you are not sure of the exact name, check how the app appears in your Start menu, Launchpad, or app drawer.

## Turn voice commands on or off

1. Open the settings panel (click the **···** button).
2. Find the checkbox labeled **Voice commands ("open [app]")**.
3. Check the box to turn voice commands on. Uncheck it to turn them off.

Voice commands are turned on by default.

## When an app is not found

If OrcaVoice cannot find or open the app you named, it falls back to normal behavior. Your spoken words get pasted as text wherever your cursor is. Nothing breaks — you just get text instead of a launched app.

## Tips

- Say only one app name. "open chrome and firefox" will try to find an app called "chrome and firefox."
- The match is exact. "open chrome" works. "open the chrome browser" will try to find an app called "the chrome browser" and may fail.
- Voice commands skip text enhancement. Saying "open chrome" will never rewrite your words or send them to the grammar checker.
